Common customer segments
For banking: new applicants, newly activated accounts, dormant accounts, secondary-account users, primary-account users, customers with recurring deposits, customers with multiple bank relationships, families, and financially vulnerable customers.
For generic products: first-time versus experienced users, free versus paid, light versus power users, individual versus business, high versus low engagement, high versus low lifetime value, and recently acquired versus long-tenured customers.
Interview tip: Select one segment to prioritize. Don’t design for everyone.

D. Prioritization cheat sheet
Dimension
Question
Customer impact
How much pain does it remove?
Reach
How many customers experience the problem?
Frequency
How often does the problem occur?
Strategic fit
Does this support the business objective?
Confidence
What evidence supports our hypothesis?
Engineering complexity
How difficult is implementation and integration?
Dependencies
Do we rely on another team, vendor or payment rail?
Time to value
How quickly could customers benefit?
Risk
Could this cause financial or regulatory harm?
Reversibility
Can we roll back or adjust the decision?
Learning potential
Can a smaller experiment validate the assumption?
Common prioritization tools: RICE, impact-versus-effort, opportunity scoring, cost of delay, and weighted scoring. Don’t introduce a framework merely to sound structured; explain the decision it helps you make.
